# Interpretation of the Phase II TiRex-2 experiments

TiRex-2 is more sensitive to the newest hours within each 32-hour patch, even when every hour stays observed. It adjusts to colder temperatures once they enter the history, then substantially damps large changes in either direction. For forecasts issued before fronts, the interval misses mostly occur on the cold side of a forecast that is too warm. The tested pressure and upstream features move the forecast, with little evidence that their correct timing improves accuracy on these cases. [Figure report and cohort counts](REPORT.md)

## The 32-hour pattern survives value perturbations

The raw sensitivity is concentrated in the newest patch. Normalizing within each patch makes a second pattern visible in the older history, where the newest slots repeatedly account for a larger share of that patch’s sensitivity. With the newest patch excluded, the newest-two to mean-slot ratios were **2.15** for ordinary cases and **2.30** for pre-front cases under ±0.25 °C perturbations. With ±1 °C perturbations they were **1.75** and **1.88**. All four 95% block-bootstrap intervals were above one. The gradient ratios were 2.49 and 2.61, and the gradient peak appeared in every season and region shown. These results establish a position effect under observed-value changes. With the newest 24 hours excluded, the ordinary masking ratio was 1.87 versus the published Phase I value of about 2.0. That historical comparison uses a seeded replacement cohort because the original case files were unavailable. [Sensitivity heatmaps](01_patch_heatmaps.png) · [Perturbation controls](02_patch_controls.png) · [Season and region profiles](02_patch_strata.png)

Equal scaled changes at the embedding input moved its output about **2.25×** as much in the newest two slots as in the average slot, with the presence indicators fixed. The embedding therefore already expresses a position effect. Moving a broad cooling pulse through two patch boundaries changed the forecast smoothly, and the sign and magnitude depended on lead and pulse age. The hourly slot effect establishes sensitivity to position, while the pulse shows how that sensitivity combines across several hours in a weather-shaped perturbation. [Pulse and embedding responses](02_pulse_embedding.png)

## The model adjusts to the cold level and damps continued change

The aligned forecasts start closer to the colder observations once the front enters the history. The +6 h and +12 h starts predict some further cooling and a rebound, with a shallower trough than observed. Across the separate change cohorts, the +24 h median forecast changed by **−0.93 °C** in strong cooling and **+1.20 °C** in strong warming. The corresponding observed changes were **−6.67 °C** and **+5.98 °C**. The stable cohort stayed close to zero. The bins are defined retrospectively by observed change, so these averages describe how the model handles realized extremes. [Front alignment](03_front_alignment.png) · [Change by cohort](03_change_cohorts.png)

At starts six hours after passage, increasing the pre-front history by 3 °C with the cold segment held fixed raised mean q50 across the horizon by **0.85 °C** and increased the +24 h minus +1 h forecast swing by **0.91 °C**. Removing the recent slope with the last observation held fixed changed mean q50 by **+0.06 °C** and the swing by **−0.19 °C**. The mean-level interval for slope removal included zero. The older temperature level still influences the forecast trajectory after passage, and the latest slope has a smaller effect in this intervention. In the illustrated case, the sLSTM memory map concentrates near its diagonal and the mLSTM heads show distinct signed mixing patterns across older tokens. These maps show how the selected cells behave at 32-hour resolution. Assigning the intervention responses to a particular gate or head would require an internal intervention and replication across cases. ## Warm bias explains much of the pre-front coverage loss

At +12 h, forecasts issued before passage were **5.59 °C too warm at q50**, and **70%** of observations were below q10. Their bands averaged **7.12 °C** wide, compared with **6.17 °C** for stable starts. The direction of the misses and the warm median point to misplaced intervals as the main issue at this lead. Wider bands still left substantial cooling outside the lower tail. Coverage improved from **30%** for pre-front starts to **61%** during passage and **79%** after passage. The +12 h median and width saliency profiles have broadly similar shapes in the covered and missed cases, with a large contribution from recent observations in both. [Coverage by issuance phase](04_coverage.png) · [Covered and missed saliency](04_coverage_saliency.png)

In the historical-drop sweep, four abrupt drops widened the mean band by **1.47 °C** at 9 °C amplitude and **0.91 °C** at 15 °C. With faded endings the widening was smaller and still declined at the largest amplitude, from **0.87 °C** at 12 °C to **0.72 °C** at 15 °C. The final 24 hours were identical across the sweep, and the logged differencing branch never switched. The ending shape contributes to the width response, and the downturn persists with faded endings on the same branch. The plotted downturn is a pattern in the sweep means. ## Covariate response and forecast skill

Across 40 pre-front cases in 12 blocks, all six +12 h error contrasts between observed and shifted covariate timing were within **0.02 °C** of zero, with intervals spanning zero. The covariate gradients and finite perturbations show that the model responds to these inputs. Their correct timing yielded little detectable benefit at this lead. In the synthetic experiment the warning flag reduced q50 by about **0.64 °C at +24 h** with 16 prior flag–drop pairs. The response varied across pair counts, so the experiment establishes that the model can respond under some synthetic histories. The single constructed history per count leaves the effect of repetition itself uncertain. [Upstream comparisons](05_upstream.png) · [Synthetic warning diagnostic](05_synthetic_warning.png) · [Paired estimates](estimates.csv)

On the 127,008 saved Phase I forecasts, the MAE advantage over the same hour yesterday fell from **81.0% at +1 h** to **5.5% at +24 h**. In retrospectively stable cases, the baseline had lower +24 h MAE, **0.92 °C** versus **1.13 °C**. The 32-hour sensitivity pattern is visible across leads in the separate Phase II sample.
